\textit{
Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) is a strategic Bank Indonesia initiative
under Project Garuda. Publicly documented Project Garuda experiments focus on
wholesale CBDC, leaving a technical gap for a testable retail prototype that
combines two-tier distribution, risk-based customer due diligence, deterministic
wallet limits, and institutional supervision. This study designs and implements
a retail Digital Rupiah prototype on a permissioned Hyperledger Fabric network.
}

\textit{
The prototyping method covers requirements analysis, architecture design,
implementation, and functional and performance testing. Go chaincode stores only
KYC status, risk, due-diligence level, senior-approval status, expiry, and document
hashes; raw identity data remains in PostgreSQL. Retail wallet tiers are derived
from the approved KYC profile rather than selected by a user. BASIC, STANDARD,
and MERCHANT policies enforce per-transaction, daily outgoing, monthly outgoing,
monthly incoming, and maximum-balance limits using the Fabric transaction
timestamp. Institutional Bank Indonesia, bank, and payment-service-provider
wallets remain outside retail tiering and use system-level limits. Hyperledger
Caliper is configured for a transfer-only workload consisting of 70\% customer-to-
customer transfers, 25\% customer-to-merchant transfers, and 5\% reads.
}

\textit{
Automated tests verify tier derivation, high-risk enhanced-due-diligence rules,
incoming and outgoing limit enforcement, privacy separation, and role boundaries.
The resulting prototype demonstrates a coherent separation of responsibilities:
customers provide identity data, banks or payment service providers perform KYC,
and Bank Indonesia supervises institutions and ledger events. Performance claims
are limited to reproducible measurements produced by the specified Caliper
protocol. The prototype therefore provides an auditable technical basis for
studying retail Digital Rupiah policy, without claiming production readiness or
legal limit equivalence.
}

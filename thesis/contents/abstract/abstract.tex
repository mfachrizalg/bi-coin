\textit{
This thesis implements and tests selected KYC and AML/CFT controls in a retail
Digital Rupiah prototype using Hyperledger Fabric 2.5. Its controls-study
extension uses a fresh four-MSP network---CentralBankMSP, DistributorMSP,
ValidatorMSP, and EndUserMSP---and three Go chaincodes for monetary operations,
customer controls, and payments. Server-managed X.509 identities distinguish
the signing actor, customer subject, and wallet custodian.
}

\textit{
Synthetic unit tests and a chaincode integration smoke test pass high-risk KYC
decisions with distinct reviewer and senior approver, restriction and
evidence-based release, reserve funding, retail payment, read-only ValidatorMSP
access, and expected endorsement failures. These results establish behavior
only for the tested synthetic scenarios; they do not establish identity
authenticity, screening effectiveness, actual PPATK reporting, or institutional
compliance.
}

\textit{
As secondary performance evidence, Hyperledger Caliper tests the separate
five-organization \texttt{digital-rupiah} v3.0 baseline. Low-contention retail
transfer reaches 20 TPS, while the paired high-contention test reaches 2.01 TPS.
Those measurements do not benchmark the three-chaincode extension and do not
establish production capacity or cross-machine reproducibility.
}

\textit{
This thesis aims to build a retail
Digital Rupiah prototype on Hyperledger Fabric 2.5 that enforces selected KYC and AML/CFT compliance controls, to verify these controls functionally, and to measure the prototype's performance using Hyperledger Caliper. Its controls-study
extension uses a fresh four-organization network (CentralBankMSP, DistributorMSP,
ValidatorMSP, and EndUserMSP) and three Go chaincodes for monetary operations,
customer controls, and payments. Server-managed X.509 identities distinguish
the signing actor, customer subject, and wallet custodian.
}

\textit{
The correctness check passed all 30 functional scenarios, 296 API route-role cells, 28 request-validation cells, and 45 action-outcome cells, with no unresolved checks, and the overall classification is pass. The synthetic scenarios cover high-risk KYC
decisions with distinct reviewer and senior approver, restriction and
evidence-based release, reserve funding, retail payment, read-only ValidatorMSP
access, and expected endorsement failures. Unit-test results are not part of this record. These results establish behavior
only for the tested synthetic scenarios; they do not establish identity
authenticity, screening effectiveness, actual PPATK reporting, or institutional
compliance.
}

\textit{
As secondary performance evidence, Hyperledger Caliper measurements of the four-organization controls-study extension with one worker on one host remain TBD. Once available, those measurements will not establish production capacity or cross-machine reproducibility.
}

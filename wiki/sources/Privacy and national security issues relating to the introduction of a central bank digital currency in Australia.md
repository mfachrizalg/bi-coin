## Evidence-backed summary

- Design/methodology/approach – The paper adopts doctrinal and normative approaches for analysis of the relevant legislations. ([[.raw/papers/privacy-and-national-security-issues-relating-to-the-introduction-of-a-central-bank-digital-currency-in-australia#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Findings – The paper suggests that the relevant legislations contain undefined terms, such as “reasonable grounds”, which may give the appearance of a balanced approach to the interactions between privacy and national surveillance laws within the context of deployment of CBDCs. ([[.raw/papers/privacy-and-national-security-issues-relating-to-the-introduction-of-a-central-bank-digital-currency-in-australia#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- The paper recommends administrators need internal policies and procedures that would give clear guidance on the possible meanings of those legislative terms to achieve the balance desired between privacy and national security requirements in Australia when deploying CBDCs. ([[.raw/papers/privacy-and-national-security-issues-relating-to-the-introduction-of-a-central-bank-digital-currency-in-australia#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- It is suggested that internal policies are to base suspicion on factual basis for decision-makers to have reasonable grounds for contravening privacy legislations. ([[.raw/papers/privacy-and-national-security-issues-relating-to-the-introduction-of-a-central-bank-digital-currency-in-australia#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- This may be achieved through requiring decision-makers to justify their decisions and consider alternative options before infringing on privacy, thus enhancing accountability. ([[.raw/papers/privacy-and-national-security-issues-relating-to-the-introduction-of-a-central-bank-digital-currency-in-australia#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Furthermore, it is suggested that the transparency and traceability provided by distributed ledger technologies will compel decision-makers to assess the benefits of privacy violations against their costs, promoting a balanced approach to surveillance and personal data disclosure. ([[.raw/papers/privacy-and-national-security-issues-relating-to-the-introduction-of-a-central-bank-digital-currency-in-australia#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Originality – The originality of the paper lies in its seminal analysis of the interaction of privacy an antimoney laundering laws in the context of the introduction of a central bank digital currency. ([[.raw/papers/privacy-and-national-security-issues-relating-to-the-introduction-of-a-central-bank-digital-currency-in-australia#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).

## Method and evidence

- Design/methodology/approach – The paper adopts doctrinal and normative approaches for analysis of the relevant legislations. (PDF p. 1).
- The paper uses doctrinal methodology to interpret the relevant laws and rules used to deal with the legal issuance and usage of CBDCs (Hutchinson, 2018). (PDF p. 2).

## Limitations and cautions

- The paper argues that lack of definition of these terms, however, renders the terms ineffective, leading to a potential regulatory overreach. (PDF p. 1).
- While Australia has not as yet determined to introduce such a CBDC, (Australian Treasury and RBA, 2024), its detailed and comprehensive analysis of risk and reward is instructive. (PDF p. 1).

- A ‘blockchain’ is a particular type of data structure used in some distributed ledgers which stores and transmits data in packages called “blocks” that are connected to each other in a digital ‘chain’. ([[.raw/papers/distributed-ledger-technology-dlt-and-blockchain#Page 6|PDF p. 6]]).
- VI DISTRIBUTED LEDGER TECHNOLOGY (DLT) AND BLOCKCHAIN ([[.raw/papers/distributed-ledger-technology-dlt-and-blockchain#Page 8|PDF p. 8]]).
- For example, a new digital currency transaction would be recorded and transmitted to a network in a data block, which is first validated by network members and then linked to an existing chain of blocks in an append-only manner, thus producing a blockchain. ([[.raw/papers/distributed-ledger-technology-dlt-and-blockchain#Page 9|PDF p. 9]]).

## Method and evidence

- Information about this new data block is then shared across the entire network, containing encrypted data so transaction details are not made public, and all network participants collectively determine the block’s validity according to a pre-defined algorithmic validation method (‘consensus mechanism’). (PDF p. 13).
- A consensus mechanism is necessary to establish whether a particular transaction is legitimate or not, using a predefined specific cryptographic validation method designated for this DL. (PDF p. 18).

## Limitations and cautions

- A further challenge, particularly relevant for the area of financial markets infrastructures, are the substantial costs related to migrating existing longstanding IT systems, operational arrangements, and institutional frameworks to DLTbased infrastructure. (PDF p. 11).
- Distributed ledger systems can be open/permissionless or permissioned, and there are fundamental differences between these two types, which lead to very different risk profiles. (PDF p. 11).

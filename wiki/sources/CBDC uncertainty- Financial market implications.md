## Evidence-backed summary

- We argue that the hedging behavior of participants in the currency futures market could be more informative than CBDC uncertainty news in the presence of limited risk absorption capacity in futures markets. ([[.raw/papers/cbdc-uncertainty-financial-market-implications#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- We show that the hedging factor has a statistically significant effect on financial market risk aversion and measures of uncertainty. ([[.raw/papers/cbdc-uncertainty-financial-market-implications#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- The hedging behavior of currency futures market participants is informative of agents' reactions to the news and central bank policies around CBDC. ([[.raw/papers/cbdc-uncertainty-financial-market-implications#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Our results also show that CBDC uncertainty is a significant risk transmitter in the financial system. ([[.raw/papers/cbdc-uncertainty-financial-market-implications#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Hence, this characteristic makes the hedging factor even more important because it can directly impact risk aversion via its moderating effects, which later influence CBDC uncertainty. ([[.raw/papers/cbdc-uncertainty-financial-market-implications#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- However, we show that standard futures market hedging behavior minimizes CBDC uncertainty. ([[.raw/papers/cbdc-uncertainty-financial-market-implications#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Under a habit-based explanation of CBDC risk premium, we argue that the hedging factor influences CBDC uncertainty via its effect on risk aversion. ([[.raw/papers/cbdc-uncertainty-financial-market-implications#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).

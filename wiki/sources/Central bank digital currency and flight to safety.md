## Evidence-backed summary

- Continuing advances in payments technologies have created a pressing need for central bankers to understand the implications for monetary policy, payments eﬃciency, and ﬁnancial stability of the introduction of central bank digital currency (CBDC). ([[.raw/papers/central-bank-digital-currency-and-flight-to-safety#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- This paper will be focused speciﬁcally on the implications of CBDC issue for ﬁnancial stability. ([[.raw/papers/central-bank-digital-currency-and-flight-to-safety#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- These new features are designed to highlight how central bank action to replace physical currency with CBDC would affect the incidence of banking panics and their effects on economic welfare. ([[.raw/papers/central-bank-digital-currency-and-flight-to-safety#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- We also want to show how monetary policy, in the form of conventional interest rate policy and central bank crisis intervention, matters in the context of physical currency and CBDC regimes. ([[.raw/papers/central-bank-digital-currency-and-flight-to-safety#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- The long-run changes in the demand for physical currency relative to other means of payment are important with regard to the potential introduction of CBDC. ([[.raw/papers/central-bank-digital-currency-and-flight-to-safety#Page 2|PDF p. 2]]).
- However, of greater importance for this paper is the short-run substitution from private means of payment to central bank means of payment that results from a change in perceptions about the stability of private ﬁnancial institutions. ([[.raw/papers/central-bank-digital-currency-and-flight-to-safety#Page 2|PDF p. 2]]).
- Given this logic, it is not surprising, with rising interest among central bankers in CBDC, that there would be concern about how CBDC could lead to ﬂight-to-safety and ﬁnancial instability. ([[.raw/papers/central-bank-digital-currency-and-flight-to-safety#Page 2|PDF p. 2]]).

## Limitations and cautions

- What has evolved as the typical – though not universal – structure for central banks includes three key features: (i) a monopoly on the issue of physical currency; (ii) a constraint that the central bank’s activities are restricted to swaps of its liabilities for other assets – principally the debt of the central government; (iii) powers that permit intervention in ﬁnancial crises. (PDF p. 1).
- However, in most countries of the world, the ratio of currency to GDP has been increasing, as has been the case in Canada and the United States. (PDF p. 2).

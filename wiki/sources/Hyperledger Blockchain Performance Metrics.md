## Evidence-backed summary

- Hyperledger Blockchain Performance Metrics ABOUT HYPERLEDGER About This Paper Hyperledger (http://hyperledger.org/) is an open source effort created to advance cross-industry blockchain technologies. ([[.raw/papers/hyperledger-blockchain-performance-metrics#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- This is the first white paper from the Hyperledger Performance and Scale Working Group (https://wiki.hyperledger.org/groups/pswg/performance-and-scale-wg). ([[.raw/papers/hyperledger-blockchain-performance-metrics#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- The purpose of this document is to define the basic terms and key metrics that should be used to evaluate the performance of a blockchain and then communicate the results. ([[.raw/papers/hyperledger-blockchain-performance-metrics#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- This paper also serves as a platform-agnostic resource for technical blockchain developers and managers interested in using industrystandard nomenclature. ([[.raw/papers/hyperledger-blockchain-performance-metrics#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- While we appreciate that there may be discrete definitions for the terms “blockchain” and “Distributed Ledger Technology (DLT),” for the purposes of this paper we will treat both terms synonymously and use the term “blockchain” throughout. ([[.raw/papers/hyperledger-blockchain-performance-metrics#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- We expect that refinements to these definitions and new blockchain-specific metrics will warrant future revisions of this document. ([[.raw/papers/hyperledger-blockchain-performance-metrics#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- In future documents, the Working Group plans to discuss workloads in greater detail and to offer additional guidance on standard procedures and emerging best practices for evaluating blockchain performance. ([[.raw/papers/hyperledger-blockchain-performance-metrics#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).

## Method and evidence

- The term benchmark originally referred to marks on workbenches used to measure standard lengths of materials, or notches cut into buildings used by surveyors to measure heights. (PDF p. 3).
- For example, you can measure the performance of version 3.4 of a system, and then use that measurement as a benchmark to see if performance is better or worse in version 3.5. (PDF p. 3).

# Investigating privacy concerns and trust in the digital Euro in Germany

- DOI: `10.1016/j.elerap.2022.101158`

## Evidence-backed summary

- Empirical results indicate that multiple antecedents are associated with privacy concerns in the digital euro that in turn influence intention to adopt a digital euro. ([[.raw/papers/investigating-privacy-concerns-and-trust-in-the-digital-euro-in-germany#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Especially soft trust factors such as credibility and image are found to influence both privacy concerns and the intention to adopt a digital euro. ([[.raw/papers/investigating-privacy-concerns-and-trust-in-the-digital-euro-in-germany#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- The results provide valuable insights for researchers and practitioners aiming at designing and implementing CBDCs by demonstrating which factors need to be considered in order to achieve widespread adoption by citizens. ([[.raw/papers/investigating-privacy-concerns-and-trust-in-the-digital-euro-in-germany#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Covid-19 led to an increase in adoption of contactless payment options, for instance in China (CNNIC, 2019, 2021) and a drop of 20–25% drop in cash use in the Euro area (Panetta, 2021). ([[.raw/papers/investigating-privacy-concerns-and-trust-in-the-digital-euro-in-germany#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Central banks (CBs) did respond to these changes by increasing their attention towards Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDC). ([[.raw/papers/investigating-privacy-concerns-and-trust-in-the-digital-euro-in-germany#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Various central banks are researching and piloting CBDC solutions for both interbank payments and settlements as well as general purpose solutions used as digital cash equivalents for end-users with “central banks collectively representing a fifth of the world’s population are likely to issue a general purpose CBDC in the next three years” (Boar et al., 2020, p.7). ([[.raw/papers/investigating-privacy-concerns-and-trust-in-the-digital-euro-in-germany#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- CBDC have the potential to not only reshape the monetary system, but to also have an impact on the level of privacy in payments for in­ dividuals. ([[.raw/papers/investigating-privacy-concerns-and-trust-in-the-digital-euro-in-germany#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).

## Method and evidence

- A quantitative survey with 1034 respondents was analyzed using partial least squares structural equation model (PLS-SEM). (PDF p. 1).
- The European Central Bank (ECB) is presently investigating the introduction of a digital euro, and conducted a survey among in­ dividuals, experts and organizations on the perception of stakeholders of such a digital euro. (PDF p. 1).

## Limitations and cautions

- However, data protection, privacy and trust play an essential role in the monetary system, with privacy being identified as a crucial element in the creation of a CBDC (Auer and Böhme, 2020). (PDF p. 1).
- However, prior work does not link trust to privacy concerns, does not tie it to a specific CBDC and makes no differentiation between different factors and types of trust that might influence in­ dividuals’ willingness to use and adopt CBDC in the future. (PDF p. 2).

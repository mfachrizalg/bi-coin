# A blueprint for energy systems in the era of central bank digital currencies

## Evidence-backed summary

- We show how this integration could increase transparency, reduce costs, trigger bottom-up citizen-driven energy initiatives, facilitate whole-system regulatory oversight, enhance carbon tracking and trading, and support energy project financing, creating a wealth of opportunities for innovation, financial inclusion in energy investment, and energy consumer engagement. ([[.raw/papers/a-blueprint-for-energy-systems-in-the-era-of-central-bank-digital-currencies#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- In October 2020, the Bahamas was the first state to introduce a central bank digital currency (CBDC) (Alonso et al., 2021), i.e. electronic cash issued by a central bank to complement physical banknotes. ([[.raw/papers/a-blueprint-for-energy-systems-in-the-era-of-central-bank-digital-currencies#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Other countries, such as Canada, India, and Australia are currently developing their CBDC, while the European central bank (ECB) expects to move to the implementation phase of the digital Euro by the end of 2023 (Deutsche Bundesbank, 2022). ([[.raw/papers/a-blueprint-for-energy-systems-in-the-era-of-central-bank-digital-currencies#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Centralised solutions can achieve greater transaction throughput and easier interoperability (Zhang and Huang, 2022), while decentralised approaches may offer better resilience (being distributed systems), and if implemented through blockchains, they can leverage the programmability of smart contracts to unlock innovation (Purusottama and Berenika Kadarusman, 2022) and facilitate integration within newer contexts lik ([[.raw/papers/a-blueprint-for-energy-systems-in-the-era-of-central-bank-digital-currencies#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- For these reasons, this Perspective focuses on blockchain-based CBDCs. ([[.raw/papers/a-blueprint-for-energy-systems-in-the-era-of-central-bank-digital-currencies#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- A blockchain is a cryptographically secure distributed ledger that ensures data integrity, system resilience, and payment traceability (Bashir, 2020). ([[.raw/papers/a-blueprint-for-energy-systems-in-the-era-of-central-bank-digital-currencies#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).
- Recent technical solutions, such as permissioned blockchains (Androulaki et al., 2018), also allow individual privacy and transactional transparency to be balanced. ([[.raw/papers/a-blueprint-for-energy-systems-in-the-era-of-central-bank-digital-currencies#Page 1|PDF p. 1]]).

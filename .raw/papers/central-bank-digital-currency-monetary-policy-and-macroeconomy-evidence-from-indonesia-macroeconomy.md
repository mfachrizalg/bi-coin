ABSTRACT
This paper studies the impact of a universally accessible interest-bearing Central
Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) on macroeconomic stability and monetary policy in
Indonesia. We construct a simple reduced-form New Keynesian model calibrated with
monthly data for Indonesia from July 2005 to February 2022. We find that the CBDC
regime significantly improves macroeconomic stability in Indonesia. In this regard,
the CBDC rate, as an additional monetary policy instrument, responsively reacts to
macroeconomic fluctuations and significantly impacts the demand side. Our results
also suggest that monetary policy is more effective under the CBDC regime than under
the non-CBDC.
Keywords: Central bank digital currency; Monetary policy; Macroeconomy; Indonesia.

I. INTRODUCTION
The governor of the Central Bank of Indonesia (Bank Indonesia hereafter), Perry
Warjiyo, has made a public statement about the intention of Bank Indonesia to
issue Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC hereafter) in the future (Reuters,
2021). Bank Indonesia is currently organizing internal research to address this
issue. With an intention to accelerate the issuance of CBDC, Bank Indonesia now
prioritizes the digital payment advancement policy, supporting payment system
digitalization. Such an intention is reflected by, first, the issuance of QR Code
Indonesian Standard (QRIS) as a uniform digital-based transaction and BI-Fast
to facilitate costless digital-based banking transactions. Second, Bank Indonesia
has also initiated the National Working Group to Accelerate and Expand Local
Digitalization (P2DD). This working group specifically encourages the national
and local level government to be proactive in promoting digital-based transactions
in the local area. Accordingly, a two-digit growth rate characterizes digital
transactions in Indonesia over the last few years. In May 2021, Bank Indonesia
documented a 57.38 percent year-on-year growth in electronic money transactions
worth 23.7 trillion transaction increases. In the digital banking sector, the volume
and value of transactions also increased significantly by 56.49 percent and 66.41
percent, respectively (Bank Indonesia, 2021). In the second quarter of 2022, the
growth of digital transactions in Indonesia remains high, with a 39.85 percent
year-on-year growth in electronic money transactions and a 38.45 percent increase
in digital banking transactions (Bank Indonesia, 2022).
Concerns about the issuance of CBDC in Indonesia emerge, we argue, three
fascinating questions, especially if the CBDC is universally accessible and interestbearing.1 The first question that appears is related to whether or not a universally
accessible interest-bearing CBDC supports Bank Indonesia’s objective to manage
macroeconomic stability. This question is central to be addressed in the case of
Indonesia as Juhro (2021) specifically mentions that Bank Indonesia always
pays attention to the potential implications of issuing CBDC on macroeconomic
stability. Furthermore, we ask how the interest rate embedded in CBDC, as a
policy instrument, will respond to and play a role in stabilizing macroeconomic
fluctuations. Bordo and Levin (2017) explain that the interest rate binding to the
CBDC provides a secure store of value of money and can serve as the main tool
for conducting monetary policy. In this case, the CBDC rate could act as a business
cycle stabilizer through which the central bank conducts the price-based CBDC
policy in a countercyclical manner (Barrdear and Kumhof, 2021; Bindseil, 2020;
Meaning et al., 2021) . Lastly, we also look into whether the existence of CBDC
enhances the effectiveness of the monetary policy. Mancini-Griffoli et al. (2018)
suggest that the interest rate channel may be the most affected and could be
strengthened by the presence of a universally accessible interest-bearing CBDC
because changes in interest rate directly affect every economic agent.

Any study elaborating on the issues of CBDC in the context of Indonesia,
unfortunately, has not formally addressed the macroeconomic stability and
monetary policy implication due to the issuance of CBDC. Harahap et al.
(2017) explore the effect of CBDC on monetary policy and macroeconomy by
emphasizing the financial technology improvement due to the CBDC. By using the
Computational General Equilibrium (CGE) simulation, they find that the issuance
of CBDC will initially increase the deposit rate, increases national and regional
economic growth, and increases the employment rate. However, none of their
findings address the macroeconomic stability impact of the CBDC in Indonesia.
Purnawan and Riyanti (2019) elaborate on the effect of CBDC on the central bank’s
policy design qualitatively and argue that CBDC provides several benefits such as
a new monetary policy instrument, increasing financial inclusion, and reducing
transaction costs. More recently, Zams et al. (2020) analyze the optimal CBDC
design for Indonesia by collecting experts’ opinions. They argue that, based on
Analytical Network Process (ANP) analysis, non-interest-bearing CBDC is the
most ideal type for Indonesia.
In this paper, we attempt to investigate the implication of a universally
accessible interest-bearing CBDC on macroeconomic stability and monetary
policy in Indonesia. To this end, we first analyze the magnitude of macroeconomic
fluctuations in response to the demand and supply shocks under the CBDC
regime and non-CBDC regime. Second, we scrutinize the role of the interest rate
embedded in CBDC as a policy instrument in response to macroeconomic shocks.
Third, we assess and compare the effectiveness of monetary policy in the CBDC
regime and the non-CBDC regime. To accommodate our objectives, we construct
a simple reduced-form New Keynesian (NK) model calibrated to the monthly data
for Indonesia from July 2005 to February 2022. We characterize the existence of a
universally accessible interest-bearing CBDC by introducing the CBDC policy rate
which is conducted separately from the conventional policy rate. In this regard,
we specify the CBDC rate reaction function using a countercyclical rule following
Barrdear and Kumhof (2021).
We conjecture that the presence of a universally accessible interest-bearing
CBDC enhances the macroeconomic stability and monetary policy effectiveness
in Indonesia. The transmission is quite straightforward. Changes in the interest
rate embedded in the CBDC influence the income of its holders. The higher
(lower) interest rate on CBDC increases the return of holding CBDC and reduces
transaction costs. For households, this increases (decreases) household income
and increases (decreases) the ability to consume. Consequently, a higher (lower)
interest rate on CBDC makes CBDC more (less) attractive. It in turn injects
(withdraws) liquidity into the economy, expanding (contracting) the demand-side
economic activity (Barrdear and Kumhof, 2021; Bordo and Levin, 2017; Gross and
Schiller, 2020; Nebbi et al., 2022). Furthermore, the effectiveness of the monetary
policy, we argue, will improve under the CBDC regime. This is because the CBDC
rate directly affects every economic agent not only the banking sector but also
households and firms. This makes the impact of changes in the CBDC rate more
directly connected to the real sector under the CBDC regime (Copic and Franke,
2020; Nebbi et al., 2022).

This paper contributes to the current literature by highlighting fresh insights
in the context of Indonesia. We find that the CBDC regime significantly improves
macroeconomic stability in Indonesia. Our result indicates that macroeconomic
shocks are significantly less volatile and more quickly stabilized under the
CBDC regime than in the non-CBDC regime. In this regard, the CBDC rate, as
an additional monetary policy instrument, responsively reacts to macroeconomic
fluctuations and significantly impacts the demand side. Our results also suggest
that the sacrifice ratio, as the proxy of monetary policy effectiveness, produced
under the CBDC regime due to discretionary policy shocks is significantly lower
than in the non-CBDC regime – meaning that the output loss due to a reduction
in the inflation rate is relatively small under the CBDC regime. This indicates that
monetary policy is more effective under the CBDC regime than under the nonCBDC.
As our objective is only to investigate the macroeconomic stability implications
of CBDC issuance in Indonesia, we argue that a simple macroeconomic model
is sufficient. However, there is a lot of room for exploration that this paper has
not mentioned in detail. First, this paper addresses the heterogeneous household
preferences in money holding and the network effect. By addressing this aspect,
we acknowledge that it would deliver a richer analysis regarding the magnitude
of the impact of issuing a universally accessible interest-bearing CBDC. For
instance, Agur, Ari, and Dell’Ariccia (2022) demonstrate that the amount of CBDC
held by the household may be heterogenous which is dependent on the degree
of anonymity preference. Also, the network effect plays an important role in
determining the degree of CBDC useability and, therefore, its consequences on the
economy. Second, this paper addresses the explicit aspect of the market structure
of the banking sector. As is illustrated by numerous works in the CBDC-related
literature, the structure of the banking sector may also play an important role
in the transmission mechanism of the CBDC rate (Andolfatto, 2018; FernándezVillaverde, Sanches, Schilling, and Uhlig, 2020; Keister and Sanches, 2019; Kim
and Kwon, 2019).
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II elaborates on the
literature review. Section III demonstrates extending the NK model. Section IV
shows the data profile and calibration parameters. Sections V and VI elaborate on
the findings and robustness tests. Section VII provides concluding remarks.
II. LITERATURE REVIEW
Discourse on CBDC has drawn significant attention from central bankers. Such
attention has been primarily driven by the potential enhancement of monetary
policy, financial stability, financial inclusion, and payment efficiency. A survey
conducted by Codruta and Wehrli (2021) illustrates that 52 out of 60 observed
central banks are now exploring the benefits and drawbacks of the CBDC issuance,
conducting experimentation, and implementing the CBDC pilot project.
The term CBDC belongs to several concepts but in general Meaning et al. (2021)
formally define CBDC as “any electronic, fiat liability of a central bank that can be used
to settle payments, or as a store of value.” This implies that CBDC virtually already
exists in the current system, namely central bank reserves. More specifically, Bech

two general categories. The first concept, the so-called universally accessible
CBDC, proposes a widely accessible CBDC to the end-users. The second one, the
so-called wholesale CBDC, suggests a narrowly provided CBDC only to particular
institutions such as commercial banks and non-bank financial institutions.
Furthermore, both types of CBDC, universally accessible and wholesale, can also
be interest-bearing or not. Nowadays, wholesale CBDC is widely considered to be
issued in advanced economies, while emerging economies are more focused on
developing universally accessible CBDC (Maryaningsih, Nazara, Kacaribu, and
Juhro, 2022).
In this regard, a universally accessible interest-bearing CBDC is certainly the
most conceptually fascinating, especially for developing and emerging countries.
This is because a universally accessible interest-bearing CBDC allows the citizens
to access the central bank directly (Bech and Garratt, 2017; Fernández-Villaverde
et al., 2020; Kiff et al., 2020). This assembles a very different circumstance from
the current system and delivers substantial macroeconomic implications. On the
other hand, wholesale CBDC relatively brings no significant consequences on
monetary policy and financial stability. Bindseil (2020) points out that wholesale
CBDC would not change the size of the monetary base so that it has no significant
practical differences from the conventional system.
A growing number of literature has drawn significant concern on the
macroeconomic implications of CBDC. Chen and Siklos (2022) argue that the
CBDC will have a significant impact on the inflation rate since, principally, its
introduction raises the speed and frequency of cash-like transactions. In this
regard, they use McCallum’s monetary rule framework and illustrate a robust
finding that the presence of CBDC will not lead to loss of inflation control, but
otherwise. Syarifuddin and Bakhtiar (2022) demonstrate that the presence of CBDC
provides several macroeconomic benefits. More specifically, they find that CBDC
offers a lower transaction cost that encourages household consumption and a
steady path leading to lower inflation. Barrdear and Kumhof (2021) investigate the
consequences of the introduction of interest-bearing CBDC using the United States
(US) data before 2008 and find that it significantly contributes to macroeconomic
stability, long-run economic growth, and inflation rate. More specifically, they
find that the interest-bearing CBDC issuance equivalent to 30 percent of the US
GDP increases the long-run GDP by 3 percent due to a lower interest rate, lessened
tax distortion, and reduction in monetary transaction cost. Furthermore, they also
find that, in the short-run, the presence of universally accessible interest-bearing
CBDC enhances business cycle stability.
The macroeconomic stability implications of CBDC can not be separated
from the role of a universally accessible interest-bearing CBDC as a monetary
policy tool. In this regard, it delivers a huge implication on monetary policy
and enhances the central bank’s control over macroeconomic stability. Bordo
and Levin (2017) explain that the interest rate embedded in the CBDC provides
a secure store of value of money and can serve as the main tool for conducting
monetary policy. In this case, the CBDC rate could act as a business cycle stabilizer
through which the central bank conducts either the quantity-based or the pricebased CBDC policy in a countercyclical manner (Barrdear and Kumhof, 2021;

Following Barrdear and Kumhof (2021), the CBDC rate reaction function is
specified using a countercyclical rule through which the CBDC rate responds
negatively to inflation and the output gap. This suggests that a countercyclical
rule of CBDC policy rate works: when the inflation rises (falls), the central bank
would respond to lower (higher) the interest rate on CBDC, making it less (more)
attractive. In turn, it withdraws (injects) liquidity from the economy and helps
to lower (raise) the output and inflation. Consequently, the economic agents
convert their CBDC to other risk-free assets and rebalance their consumption and
investments.
Our primary analysis relies on a simple procedure in which we compare and
evaluate the macroeconomic consequences that arose from the exogenous shocks
εit+1,i∈{y,π,icb,idc} under the non-CBDC and CBDC regimes. Under the CBDC
regime, the central bank has two complementing policy instruments and responds
to macroeconomic shocks by adjusting the policy rate and the CBDC rate. On
the other hand, the central bank only has the policy rate as the monetary policy
instrument under the non-CBDC regime. This makes the model of the non-CBDC
regime a standard NK three-equation model.

To address our research questions, we first analyze the magnitude of
macroeconomic fluctuations in response to the demand and supply shocks
under the CBDC regime and non-CBDC regime. Second, we scrutinize the role
of the interest rate embedded in CBDC as a policy instrument in response to
macroeconomic shocks. Third, we investigate the effectiveness of monetary policy
in the CBDC regime and the non-CBDC regime. For this end, we construct a simple
reduced-form New Keynesian (NK) model calibrated to Indonesia’s monthly
data. We characterize the existence of a universally accessible interest-bearing
CBDC by introducing the CBDC policy rate which is conducted separately from
the conventional policy rate. In this regard, we specify the CBDC rate reaction
function using a countercyclical rule in a tradition of Barrdear and Kumhof (2021).
This paper contributes to the current literature by highlighting fresh
insights for Indonesia. We find that the CBDC regime significantly improves
macroeconomic stability in Indonesia. Our result indicates that macroeconomic
shocks are significantly less volatile and more quickly stabilized under the CBDC
regime than under the non-CBDC regime. In this regard, the CBDC rate, as an
additional monetary policy instrument, responsively reacts to macroeconomic
fluctuations and significantly impacts the demand side. Our results also suggest
that the sacrifice ratio, as the proxy of monetary policy effectiveness, produced
under the CBDC regime due to discretionary policy shocks is significantly lower
than in the non-CBDC regime – meaning that the output loss due to a reduction
in the inflation rate is relatively small under the CBDC regime. This indicates that
monetary policy is more effective under the CBDC regime than in the non-CBDC.
Our findings are robust to sensitivity checks using the alternative value of several
key parameters.
Based on our results, we suggest that policymakers, especially Bank
Indonesia, need to consider issuing a universally accessible interest-bearing CBDC
in Indonesia. Our results show that it brings several economic benefits, from
macroeconomic stability to a lower degree of sacrifice ratio. However, this requires
robust preparation, especially related to the technical aspect, cyber security aspect,
and political economy aspect. Departing from this concern, Bank Indonesia needs
to work out the long-run master plan before issuing CBDC in Indonesia.

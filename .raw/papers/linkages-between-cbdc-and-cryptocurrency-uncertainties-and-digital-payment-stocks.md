We examine the connectedness between CBDC uncertainty, cryptocurrency policy uncertainty
(UCRY policy), cryptocurrency price uncertainty (UCRY price), and digital payment stock returns
and volatility using data from November 20, 2015, to December 30, 2022. We find CBDC un­
certainty is a net recipient, while UCRY policy and UCRY price are net transmitters. Most digital
payment stocks are net recipients except for the three largest of VISA, Mastercard, and American
Express. Finally, net pairwise connectedness shows weak connectedness between uncertainties
indices and digital payment stocks, consistent with the hedging capability of digital payment
stocks against the uncertainties of CBDC and cryptocurrency markets.

1. Introduction and motivation
Technological improvement has been playing an important role in payment systems around the world in the last two decades (Agur
et al., 2022). Traditionally, the biggest players of payments markets were commercial banks and digital payment companies like VISA,
Mastercard, and American Express, etc., However, the introduction of blockchain based cryptocurrencies has changed the sphere of
payments market, especially after 2017 (Cross et al., 2021). After cryptocurrencies, the recent development in payment systems is the
launch of new central bank digital currencies (CBDC). Many nations are working on digitalizing their sovereign currencies, with the
pace of development varying across countries (Xu, 2022). Very prominently, China has launched their CBDC with the name of e-CNY,
and they are currently adding the feature of payments to their e-CNY application as of the ongoing event of the 2023 beginning of the
Chinese Lunar New Year, to facilitate citizens sharing ‘red packets’ (monetary gifts). It is expected that CBDCs will soon evolve to be
among the biggest players within the global payment markets.
Traditional digital payment companies, commercial banks, cryptocurrencies, and CBDCs are rivals in the payments market,
therefore changes in one payment market will affect other payment markets. All payment modes have advantages and disadvantages.
For example, most cryptocurrencies are decentralized, allowing business and retail customers to make payments without showing their
identity, at lower cost, and in less time compared with traditional payment companies and banks (Tsang and Yang, 2021; Yousaf et al.,
2022).
With these extra benefits, new risks are also emerging regarding cryptocurrencies, like high volatility (Dai et al., 2022; Yousaf et al.,

2023), cyberattack (Akyildirim et al., 2023), irreversible payments (Sobhanifard and Sadatfarizani, 2019), frauds (Mackenzie, 2022;
Yousaf and Goodell, 2023), regulatory uncertainty, and non-legal tender (Caporale et al., 2021; Allen et al., 2022). However, CBDCs
solve the issue of legal tender (Bian et al., 2021), but these currencies will be centrally controlled by central banks and there are
concerns about the data privacy in CBDCs (Jabbar et al., 2023).
Traditional information channels, as well as social media platforms share various kinds of positive and negative information about
the developments in these payment markets, which ultimately shape the perception of business and retail payment customers about
their preferences for these payment systems. Wang et al. (2022) recently introduced the CBDC uncertainty index formed from
traditional and social media news data regarding the CBDCs. This index gauges the uncertainty in the CBDC market. They examine the
impact of CBDC uncertainty on bitcoin, banks, bonds, stock, gold, and currency markets, finding that CBDC uncertainty is negatively
associated with the volatility of stock market and banking sector, whereas negatively linked with the volatility of gold, bond, fiat
currency, and cryptocurrency markets. Additionally, Lucey et al. (2022) propose two indices, one for cryptocurrency policy uncer­
tainty (UCRY policy), and another for cryptocurrency price uncertainty (UCRY price), to measure the news-based uncertainties of the
cryptocurrency market. Several studies use these three uncertainty indices of CBDC and cryptocurrencies. Hassan et al. (2021) examine
the dynamic relationship between UCRY policy, UCRY price, and precious metal markets, and report gold as strong safe haven against
the uncertainties of cryptocurrency markets. Wei et al. (2022) find that UCRY policy and price indices are useful in forecasting the
metal’s volatility. Yan et al. (2022) examine the impact of UCRY policy and price indices on high and low carbon based mutual fund’s
investment flows, and find that the cryptocurrency uncertainties influence the high carbon mutual fund’ investment flows. Wang et al.
(2022) find the positive association between UCRY indices and the newly introduced cryptocurrency environmental attention index.
Hasan et al. (2022) find that Sukuk, gold, and Islamic indices are strong hedge against the policy related uncertainty of cryptocurrency
market. Shang et al. (2022) use the Occam’s window approach and find that UCRY policy is useful in forecasting gold returns.
The current study contributes to above mentioned literature of CBDC and cryptocurrency uncertainty indices by examining their
relationship with traditional digital payment companies’ stocks. More specifically, we examine the relationship between CBDC un­
certainty, UCRY policy, UCRY price, and digital payments stock’ returns and volatility using the TVP-VAR model. Apart from the
linkages between CBDC and cryptocurrency uncertainty indices, surprisingly we find no study on the cryptocurrency-digital payment
stocks nexus, therefore this study provides useful insights to investors, regulators, and policy makers of CBDCs, cryptocurrencies, and
digital payment companies regarding portfolio construction, hedging, financial stability, and making regulations.
We find that uncertainty indices are more connected with returns compared to the volatility of digital payment stocks. Examination
of static and dynamic connectedness shows that CBDC uncertainty is a net recipient of spillover from digital payment stocks, whereas
UCRY policy and UCRY price are clearly transmitters of spillovers to digital payment stocks. Digital payment stocks are typically net
recipients throughout the sample period. Exceptions to this, however, are the three largest payment stocks of VISA, Mastercard, and
American Express. Finally, net pairwise connectedness analysis reveals weak connectedness between uncertainty indices and digital
payment stocks, showing the hedging capability of digital payment stocks against the uncertainties of CBDC and cryptocurrency
markets.
The rest of the study is structured as follows: Section 2 describes the methodology, Section 3 shares results, while Section 4
concludes.
2. Data and methodology
Following Wang et al. (2022), and Lucey et al. (2022), we use the weekly data of three uncertainty indices of CBDC and crypto­
currency markets. These indices include the CBDC Uncertainty Index (CBDC-U), the Cryptocurrency Policy Uncertainty Index (UCRY
Policy), and the Cryptocurrency Price Uncertainty Index (UCRY Price). The data of uncertainty indices is extracted from the website of
Digital Assets Indices.1
Weekly data of ten highly capitalized digital payments stocks are collected from investing.com. These include Visa-Visa Inc, MAMastercard Incorporated, AXP-American Express Company, PYPL-PayPal Holdings Inc, FISV- Fiserv Inc, MELI- MercadoLibre Inc, SQBlock Inc, FIS- Fidelity National Information Services Inc, WU- Western Union Company, and Tencent. The sample period is November
20, 2015–December 30, 2022.2

2022). Further, the TVP-VAR model is useful in estimating the static and dynamic total, TO, FROM, and NET spillovers. The TVP-VAR

3. Empirical findings

Table 1 reports descriptive statistics of uncertainty indices and digital payment stocks. SQ and MELI stocks provide the highest
returns whereas WU’s returns are negative. With respect to unconditional volatility, the highest volatility is manifested by SQ and
MELI, while the lowest volatility for VISA. Regarding the uncertainty indices, the mean and standard deviations are highest for UCRY
policy, and lowest for CBDC uncertainty. Further, significant Jarque-Bera and Augmented Dicky Fuller/Phillps-Perron coefficients
indicate that all series are normally distributed and stationary.
Table 2 presents the unconditional correlations between the uncertainty indices and the digital payment stocks. CBDC uncertainty,
UCRY policy, and UCRY price are negatively correlated with all digital payment stocks except for Tencent. Table 3 consists of
connectedness between uncertainty indices and returns of digital payment stocks. Results indicate a total connectedness of 62.9%,
showing a moderate level of association between uncertainty indices and digital payment stocks.
Fig. 1 highlights that the total connectedness between uncertainty indices and returns of digital payment stocks is time varying. An
abrupt increase in connectedness is observed at the first quarter of 2020, coinciding with the COVID-19 pandemic. This result is
consistent with Karomer et al. (2022) who also evidence higher connectedness between uncertainty indices and cryptocurrency market
during COVID-19. The ‘To’ and ‘From’ spillovers, denoted in blue in Table 3, are small, showing weak connectedness between un­
certainty indices and digital payment stock returns.
The net spillover of CBDC uncertainty is − 13.38, indicating CBDC uncertainty as a net recipient of spillover from the system.
However, the net spillovers of UCRY policy and UCRY price are 1.94 and 1.08 respectively, consistent with being net transmitters of
spillover to the system. This suggests that uncertainty of cryptocurrency market, either policy or price based, is useful for forecasting
the movements of digital payment stocks. This result is consistent with Shang et al. (2022) who find that UCRY policy is useful in
forecasting returns of gold.
With respect to digital payment stocks, the strongest transmitters and recipients of spillovers are VISA (25.53) and Tencent
(− 20.25). Finally, the time-varying results of net spillovers between uncertainty indices and digital payments stock returns are

4. Conclusions
Central bank digital currencies, cryptocurrencies, and digital payments companies are various alternative payment vehicles, but no
studies have explored the interlinkages among them in past literature. Therefore, we investigate the connectedness between central
bank digital currency uncertainty (CBDC uncertainty), cryptocurrency policy uncertainty (UCRY policy), cryptocurrency price un­
certainty (UCRY price), and the returns and volatility of ten highly capitalized digital payment stocks using the TVP-VAR approach.
The total spillover indices show that uncertainty indices are more connected with returns compared to the volatility of digital
payment stocks. Static and dynamic connectedness shows that CBDC uncertainty is a net recipient, with risk and returns of digital
payments stocks influencing the CBDC related uncertainty. Policymakers can consider the influence of global digital payment com­
panies as an important factor for the uncertainty of CBDCs. However, the UCRY policy and UCRY price are clear transmitters of
spillovers to digital payment stock returns and volatilities, implying that cryptocurrency related uncertainty strongly influences digital
payment stocks.
Results suggest that cryptocurrency market uncertainties are useful for forecasting digital payment stock returns and volatility.
Most digital payment stocks are net recipients throughout the sample period, with exceptions being the three largest payment stocks
VISA, Mastercard, and American Express. As crypto market uncertainty influences the digital payment stocks, policymakers should
continuously monitor the movements of cryptocurrencies and design policies/regulations accordingly to stabilize the conventional
payment markets. Further, net pairwise connectedness analysis reveals weak connectedness between uncertainties indices and digital
payment stocks. Results imply that digital payments stocks are very useful for mitigating the uncertainties of both CBDCs and cryp­
tocurrency markets. Results indicate the utility of cryptocurrency investors including digital payment stocks in their portfolios to
diversify the risk of cryptocurrencies.

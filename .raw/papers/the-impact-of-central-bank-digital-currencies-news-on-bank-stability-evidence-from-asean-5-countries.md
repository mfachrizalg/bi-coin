ABSTRACT
This study constructs an index reflecting Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)
news coverage and evaluates its impact on bank stability. The CBDC Attention Index
(CBDCAI) is formulated based on more than 533 Reuters Digital News Report articles
from 2019 to 2023. Employing the System Generalized Method of Moments (GMM)
approach with data from 92 banks across ASEAN-5 nations, and implementing quantile
regression for robustness checks, the findings demonstrate a significant relationship
between the CBDCAI and bank stability, alongside variables such as bank size and
capitalization. These results offer valuable guidance for central banks, especially
within the ASEAN-5 region, as they advance CBDC initiatives.
Keywords: CBDC; Attention index; Bank stability; News sentiment; ASEAN-5.

I. INTRODUCTION
This paper examines how news about Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)
affects banking sector stability. Early studies show that negative news can cause
emotional distress among depositors, leading to mass withdrawals. Diamond and
Dybvig (1983) highlight the strong impact of negative sentiment on bank deposit
behavior.
The motivation for this study stems from existing literature examining how
behavioral elements of news, such as sentiment, attention, and uncertainty, affect
asset pricing. For instance, Chen and Srinivasan (2019) investigate the influence
of investor attention on numerous financial assets, while Adekoya et al. (2022)
investigate the impact of Google-based investor attention on fourth industrial
revolution assets. Furthermore, several papers, including Ballinari et al. (2022),
Bijl et al. (2016), Cai et al. (2022) and Chen et al. (2013), investigate the impact of
investor sentiment and attention on stock market volatility and returns.
The influence of news concerning the digital economy and CBDCs on financial
markets has also been the subject of numerous recent studies. Wang et al. (2023)
investigate the impact of concerns regarding CBDCs on financial assets. Dunbar
(2023) look into the impact of CBDCs on bond and currency markets, while Yousaf
and Goodell (2023) examine the impact of uncertainties regarding the digital
economy and CBDCs on digital payment equities.
This study extends the existing literature by examining the influence of Central
Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) news on banking stability within the ASIAN-5
countries currently adopting this innovative monetary approach. The findings
offer valuable insights that both advance academic understanding and serve as
a meaningful resource for central banks in managing communications related to
digital currencies.
According to the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), 70% of central banks
have either announced their intentions or are currently developing a CBDC. Central
banks, representing one-fifth of the global population, are expected to implement
retail CBDCs within the next three years (Dupuis et al., 2022). As of October 6,
2023, 11 countries or territories had fully implemented CBDCs, according to the
Atlantic Council. Ten of these countries are in the Caribbean, while one is in Africa
(Nigeria). On the same date, 130 countries were in the “advanced” exploration
phase, which includes development, pilot, or launch stages. Moreover, 19 of the
20 G-20 countries were in the advanced exploration phase for CBDCs (Hoque et
al., 2024).
Nevertheless, a significant concern for banks is that CBDC holdings could
partially replace traditional deposits (Beckmann et al., 2024). The perception of
risk and stability may be altered by CBDCs, which are viewed differently from
traditional currencies and assets. As a result, CBDCs can have a notable impact
on market sentiment and asset allocation. Yang and Zhou (2022) argue that the
transmission effect of monetary policy instruments can be improved, and the
circulation of currency can be accelerated through CBDCs, which in turn affects
financial markets. Therefore, the effectiveness of monetary policy should be
assessed in relation to the impact of CBDCs on the broader financial market,
including the banking sector.

This paper offers three contributions to CBDC literature. Firstly, we establish
a CBDC index by evaluating the frequency of news coverage using media
sources (refer to section 3). Several researchers have demonstrated that the
frequency of news coverage can be used as a surrogate to measure the level of
uncertainty surrounding economic or financial objectives, such as monetary
policy, cryptocurrency policy, or cryptocurrency prices (Baker et al. (2016); Huang
and Luk (2020); Lucey et al., 2022). It can also be employed to evaluate the extent
to which the public is concerned with economic or financial objectives, including
P2P lending, the cryptocurrency environment, and cryptocurrencies (He et al.,
2022; Wang et al., 2022). These publications demonstrate that the aforementioned
uncertainty or concern indices can be used as proxies for efficiency and validity.
They accomplish this by investigating the impact of these indices on micro- or
macroeconomic variables. Our endeavor to examine the impact of CBDC news on
bank stability is motivated by the scarcity of research in this field.
Secondly, this critically elaborated the influence of CBDC news on the stability
of the financial systems of the ASEAN-5 countries. The region’s dedication to
accelerate the financial system integration to promote economic growth and
financial inclusion was evident in the 2014 ratification of the ASEAN Banking
Integration Framework (ABIF). Regional banks will be compelled to enhance their
performance by adopting ABIF, which lead to a substantial consolidation in the
banking industry and further increase competition. CBDC has the potential to
further improve financial inclusion in developing countries in Asia.
Our final contribution is to the banking-specific literature on CBDCs in the
ASEAN-5 region. The financial stability of the banking system, particularly the
impact of CBDCs, remains a topic of ongoing debate without a clear consensus.
Most existing research primarily focuses on qualitative analyses of CBDCs and
relies on simulations and theoretical models, with insufficient empirical evidence
to provide concrete insights. In this context, the current research aims to offer a
more comprehensive, data-driven analysis, thus making a significant contribution
to the ongoing discourse.
The research objectives are addressed through the implementation of
the System GMM (SGMM) methodology. This method enhances efficacy by
permitting additional instruments (Roodman, 2009). SGMM is deemed superior to
first-difference GMM by Blundell and Bond (1998) due to its reduced sample bias
and efficacy. The two-step SGMM estimator is employed with Windmeijer’s (2005)
finite sample correction, rendering it more efficient than the one-step estimator
(Roodman, 2009). Furthermore, SGMM assists in resolving the endogeneity
problem, a critical concern in banking and corporate finance. Blundell and Bond
(1998) introduced the SGMM method, which generates more efficient and resilient
outcomes. We also employ panel quantile regression analysis to guarantee that
the conclusions are robust and do not depend on the results of a singular method.
Our results indicate that the CBDCAI index has a substantial and positive
correlation with bank stability (Z-Score). Consequently, fortifying the
communication task force among the ASEAN-5 on CBDC is imperative to
guarantee that the public receives high-quality CBDC messages. The results of
this study offer novel insights that benefit the media, policymakers, regulators,
and investors regarding the evolution of CBDCs as a barometer in the new digital

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. Landscape of ASEAN-5 CBDC Development
CBDC is part of the latest digital finance development that has emerged as the
need for transactions in blockchain integration increases (Sethaput and Innet, 2023;
Zhang and Huang, 2022). International Monetary Funds (2020) defines CDBC as a
digital payment instrument that is nationally denominated by the Central Bank of
a country. The development of CBDC is inseparable from the thoughts of Friedric
Hayek, a Nobel laureate in economics who gave the idea that the government
should not monopolize the issuance of money (Hayek, 1977). Through this idea,
various innovations emerged including Bitcoin in 2008 which is a new digital
transaction tool issued by non-governmental organizations (Zams et al., 2020).
Along with the development of financial technology, more and more digital
payment instruments transform the direct use of money into a non-physical form
and have a strong user market (Sethaput and Innet, 2023).
However, not all digital money is increasingly used, although there are various
concerns including security and risk over the absence of jurisdictional guarantees
and its volatile nature makes this innovation even difficult to gain public trust
(Fadli et al., 2023). This situation provides space for authorities to issue digital
currencies that are more stable and secure in the form of CBDCs. CBDC can also
provide a solution in reducing the burden of regulation and supervision by the
central bank over digitally complex payment systems (Maryaningsih et al., 2022).
The concept of CBDC in the form of digital currency was initially developed
through the Digital Currency Electronic Payment (DCEP) project by the Central
Bank of China in 2014. While the country that officially uses CBDC is the Bahamas,
which launched Sand Dollar in 2014 (Hofmann, 2023).
CBDC provides a strong opportunity to increase the efficiency of more stable
transactions with central bank guaranteed security (Bender et al., 2022). Other
advantages offered are digital penetration that increases financial inclusion
nationwide (Sethaput and Innet, 2023), cheaper transactions (Yang et al., 2024),
simpler banking infrastructure (Wang et al., 2022) and reduced financial barriers
domestically and even between countries (Le et al., 2023). This CBDC can be
deployed in the form of wholesale CBDC which is only accessible to entities
with certain requirements such as financial institutions that can perform clearing
transactions, interbank and even the use of tokenized platforms (Luu et al., 2023a).
Another form of CBDC is retail which is a digital form of official currency and can
be widely used by the public (Baeriswyl et al., 2024).
Although the development of CBDC is mostly carried out in developed
countries, especially in the European Union, currently ASEAN countries, especially
the ASEAN-5, are also actively developing CBDC in various pilot projects and
preparing for the official issuance of CBDC. Singapore became the first ASEAN-5
country to pay intense attention to CBDC. The Monetary Authority of Singapore
(MAS) in 2016 initiated Project Ubin which aims to examine the use of blockchain

2.2. News and Financial Market
In the era of the information economy, media plays a very important role in
financial markets. Reports presented by the media can reduce information
asymmetry between companies and investors, accelerate information distribution,
and improve efficiency in pricing financial assets (Fang and Peress, 2009).
Media sentiment is a crucial factor affecting financial markets. Another line of
research suggests that investors get their information from the media, which in turn
can influence their sentiment through media coverage of economic and financial
news. Hayo and Neuenkirch (2015) found that market participants rely on media
reports to follow events related to central banks. Therefore, the media has a direct
influence on financial markets, by stimulating stock trading activity and increasing
stock price fluctuations (Peress, 2014). Pessimism spread by the media can also
lead to a drop in market prices, which is then followed by a recovery back towards
the base value (Tetlock, 2011). In addition, individual investors often overreact to
outdated news, which suggests that the media remains influential even when the
information provided is pre-existing (Tetlock, 2011). However, it should be noted

2.3. Empirical Review
The emergence of CBDC in recent years has attracted considerable interest from
scholars and economists. CBDC is a digital currency a central bank issues. The
central bank bears the obligation of issuing this currency. According to Ward and
Rochemont (2019), CBDC refers to a digital currency issued by a central bank
backed by funds held in traditional reserve or settlement accounts. Bitter (2020)
defines CBDC as a form of digital liability issued by the central bank that the
general public may access. It can bear interest and is account-based. According
to Ozili (2022), digital currency refers to digital fiat money that a central bank
issues. These definitions illustrate that CBDC, or Central Bank Digital Currency, is
a central bank liability and differs from physical cash in its tangible characteristics.
However, CBDC serves the same purpose as cash, facilitating transactions and
payments.
CBDC offers more benefits than conventional cryptocurrencies and fiat
currencies. First, CBDCs have the potential to enhance their status as legal tender,
prevent counterfeiting, and reduce transaction costs in the context of payments.
Second, CBDCs could improve the inclusiveness of the payment system (Sun et
al., 2017). By reducing transaction costs, facilitating secure payment methods,
expanding access to digital financial services, and encouraging unbanked
individuals to participate in the formal financial system, CBDCs can promote
financial inclusion. A two-tier system involving commercial banks facilitates
a more seamless integration between the central bank and the public, thereby
reducing the financial inclusion divide, particularly in developing countries (Tan,
2023).
Third, CBDCs may benefit from stricter monetary regulations. The central
bank can more accurately regulate the money supply with a structured currency
system (Agarwal et al., 2021; Fernández-Villaverde et al., 2021). This addresses
various challenges in contemporary monetary policy, including the movement
of funds from the physical economy to the digital economy, currency conversion
management, and inefficient policy transmission. Additionally, the rapid
acquisition of information regarding the movement of funds is beneficial in the
fight against corruption, money laundering, terrorism financing, and tax evasion
(Tronnier, 2021; Dupuis et al., 2022). Fourth, CBDCs can improve financial market
stability by suppressing shadow banking activities, reducing systemic risk, and
facilitating monetary adjustments (Larina and Akimov, 2020; Copeland, 2020;
Zams et al., 2020).

The fifth group examines the influence of CBDC on the monetary system and
macroeconomic stability. According to Yao et al. (2018), implementing CBDCs
in China can enhance the strength of money’s value and provide a helpful
instrument for macroeconomic regulation. Bordo and Levin (2017) argues that
CBDCs can potentially enhance the effectiveness of monetary policy. Williamson
(2022) constructed a model encompassing diverse payment instruments to assess
the influence of CBDC on payment systems. The authors demonstrate that CBDCs
effectively decrease criminal activities linked to real cash while still enabling the
payment of interest on principal liabilities of central banks. Kim and Kwon (2019)
analyze the consequences of CBDCs on financial stability by applying a monetary
general equilibrium model. They argue that implementing deposits in CBDC
accounts will decrease the availability of private credit from public banks, leading
to an increase in nominal interest rates and a decrease in the reserve-to-deposit
ratio of public banks. Minesso et al. (2022) analyze the risks and adverse effects
of CBDCs in an open economy. These studies demonstrate that the existence of
CBDCs can significantly enhance the effects of global shocks. Nevertheless, the
extent of this influence will be contingent upon the precise configuration of the
CBDC. It can be substantially diminished if the CBDC incorporates risk mitigation
characteristics.
Finally, the sixth group examine the consequences of CBDC on the banking
sector. Chiu et al. (2023) demonstrates that the presence of market power in the
deposit market among banks may be addressed by implementing interest-bearing
CBDCs. This measure would enhance competition, boost the competitiveness
of deposit rates, facilitate intermediation, and ultimately increase production.

interest-bearing CBDCs has the potential to reduce the demand for physical

3.2. Model Estimation
The stability of a bank might exhibit a persistent feature over time, meaning that
the present log Z-score value is anticipated based on its past value Bermpei et al.
(2018). Please consider lagged Z-score values to avoid the introduction of bias in
the analysis of variables. Hence, in line with other research (Bermpei et al., 2018;
Nguyen, 2021; Tran et al., 2022), this work employs a dynamic panel model that
considers the autoregressive characteristics of the Z-Score. The model has been
estimated using a two-step generalized method of moments (GMM) estimator,
which was initially developed by Arellano and Bond (1991) and later improved by
Arellano and Bover (1995) to address potential issues—linked to the phenomenon
of reverse causation between bank stability and the independent variables. The
GMM estimator is superior to conventional estimators such as Ordinary Least
Square (OLS), Least Square Dummy Variable (LSDV) and Instrument Variable
Estimator. This is because it generates consistent estimates in the presence of
unobserved heteroscedasticity, endogeneity, and autocorrelation in panel data
(Jagannathan et al., 2002). This estimator surpasses the IV estimator by obviating
the requirement for external instruments. Instead, it employs lagged values of
preset and endogenous variables as internal instruments to address potential
endogeneity effectively (Tran et al., 2022). Furthermore, it exhibits greater efficiency
and resilience to error distribution than the IV – 2SLS and 3SLS estimators (Hall,
2005).
The GMM approach is preferable due to its ability to yield more consistent and
efficient outcomes for imbalanced panel data, mainly when the number of crosssectional units (N) is large and the period (T) is small (Arellano and Bond, 1991;
Roodman, 2009). In contrast, different GMM exhibits diminished testing power—
and inadequate measurement tools (Arellano and Bover, 1995). The GMM system
concurrently evaluates the similarity in level and the difference between two
variables. More precisely, equations at different levels are modified by taking the
first lag difference of the studied variable. On the other hand, the first difference
equations are modified by using the levels of the delayed variable. It is important
to note that the instruments used in these modifications are not correlated with
the fixed effects Roodman (2009). The two-step system GMM estimator is more

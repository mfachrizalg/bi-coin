Abstract—Blockchain has become a revolutionary solution in
different sectors to provide improved security, shareability, and
decentralization. Concerns about data leakage, lack of trust, and
central points of weakness that came up because of cloud
computing are given new big chances to deal with them.
However, this concept has drawbacks related to latency,
throughput, and resource utilization, which affect system
performance. Lastly, this paper does a thorough review of the
previous research that looked at how blockchain adapts to cloud
integration from the points of view of performance and
architectural improvements, as well as its uses. It is possible to
find the main publication sources for a wider range of papers.
The focus is on new topics, like hybrid architectures, efficient
consensus algorithms, and lightweight protocols. The
implications of these results are perhaps most evident in the
requirement for multi-disciplinary action on scalability and
efficiency. In addition, this review gives direction for future work
and comes up with the notion that energy-efficient mechanisms,
real-time analytics, and improvements to the current standards
of interoperability are needed for the development of blockchain
in cloud environments to be optimized further.

I. INTRODUCTION
Today there is a new trend implying the usage of remote
computing resources through the Internet cloud solutions,
which became popular due to their scalability and flexibility, as
well as the opportunities that help avoid significant costs for IT
infrastructure [1]. Nonetheless, issues like control of the
organization centre, data violation, and less visibility have
inspired search for better technologies to fix these issues [2].
Blockchain, a distributed and highly secure and practically
tamper-proof methodology that maintains a record of
transactions, appears as the answer [3], [4].
It is explained that connectivity to the cloud computing can
promote system safety, openness, and credibility while it brings
main performance issues including latency, throughput, and
resource utilization [5], [6] and [7]. Latency is one of the key
factors, which quantifies the time delay for the validation of
transaction [8], [9]. Block chain-based cloud systems some
important consensus like PoW and PoS also have great
influence on latency that hinders real-time applications in [11],
[12].

III. PERFOMRANCE IMPLICATION
The latency that arises from the execution delay and data
propagation in consensus poses a significant challenge to
interoperating blockchain with cloud technologies [11], [13].
As the number of consensus mechanism demands significant
amount of computation, platforms such as Hyperledger Fabric
[19] show the tradeoff between latency vs performance. Since
latency issues are very important in case of use cases dealing

with real time applications like verification of IoT transactions,
one needs to understand the reason for this. It has been also
proven that latency still remains a barrier in scattered situations
[19], and that the Proof of Authority (PoA) consensus in
particular can minimize latency. For performing cases where
speed is more important than decentralization, PoA proves
better as it validates transactions faster than the rest methods
[23].
In general, throughput, expressed through transactions per
second (TPS) is an important metric to assess the scalability of
the blockchain technology. In case of heavy workloads, private
blockchain networks have also shown notable throughput
limitations [1]. This limits its application in such sectors where
massive data is required such as in financial systems and
Supply Chain Management [14]. Therefore, the challenge has
been introduced in parallelized transaction processing. Parallel
processing improves throughput and decreases processing
duration, and so increases the scalability of blockchain systems
[17], [18], according to Giampaolo et al. [15].
The resource usage of systems based on Proof of Work
increases due to the distributed nature of blockchain, such as
large computational power, energy, storage [30]. Thus, cloud
integration has issues when resources are often limited [31],
[32]. Thus, lightweight blockchain protocols have been
developed to decrease resource consumption. Sharma et al.
[22] designed a protocol for electronic health records (EHR)
management to both safeguard data confidentiality effectively
and consume low resources. Fusion of blockchain and cloud
continues to be about striking an optimal balance between
enhancing the performance efficiency with energy saving [24].
Addressing these challenges will increase the latency,
throughput, resource management, and can increase the
application dimensions of blockchain in many sectors [25],
[27].
IV. ARCHITECTURAL OPTIMIZATIONS
A successful approach to resolve performance challenge
has been integration of blockchain with cloud platform [23],
[24], [25]. Edge computing, as an approach for improving
throughput and lowering latency has been incorporated as one
embedded approach in Blockchain Enabled Cloud Architecture
(BECA), e.g. [26].
Based on hybrid architecture, distributed computation is
developed among which edge devices become less subject to
centralized data centers, and real time processing can be
achieved as a result. Ajayi et al., [26] found that these
architectures are particularly beneficial for latency-sensitive
applications such as IoT and healthcare, where they combine
the global scalability of cloud systems with the security and
decentralization of blockchain [27].
Innovative consensus mechanisms are essential to
improving blockchain scalability and efficiency in cloud
environments [28], [29]. Delegated Proof of Stake (DPoS)
enhances transaction validation by assigning preselected
validators to the consensus process, thereby maintaining
decentralization while significantly increasing throughput [8].
Similarly, Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) mechanisms

VII. CONCLUSION
The combination of blockchain and cloud computing is
therefore promising a more transformative shift in various
fields, especially where issues with security, transparency, and
scalability are core. However, latency, low throughput, and
high resource utilization continue to be core hurdles to
overcome. New trends in architectural development, consensus
algorithms, and combined approaches offer promising signals
to these challenges. We can see the combination of edge
computing solutions with newer consensus algorithms like PoS
and BFT as pathways to optimization and growth.
In addition, the problems with performance call for the
development of more energy-efficient protocols and real-time
analysis techniques. More such endeavors and interdisciplinary
work will be helpful in rising above these issues and enabling
the blockchain-cloud ecosystems. This will help extend the
application of blockchain in various sectors, promote new
development in the field, and improve systemization.

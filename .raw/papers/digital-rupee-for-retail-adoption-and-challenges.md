Abstract— Central Bank Digital Currency or CBDC is a
digital currency that central banks issue. The Reserve Bank
of India launched its version of digital currency called Digital
Rupee in the latter half of 2022 as a pilot project. This paper's
scope is with one of the types of Digital Rupee i.e. Digital
Rupee for Retail (Retail CBDC). This research will cover the
Digital Rupee’s adoption, sentiment, and challenges among
citizens. In this paper, we researched retail consumer's
adoption rate based on demography, awareness of this
initiative, perceived benefits and challenges, and options for
this digital currency's future adoptions. In the final results,
there is low adoption among Indian citizens and three main
reasons first Digital Rupee is still in the pilot stage and the
government is solving daily challenges that it brings, second,
there is a lack of awareness among participants and third
participants who have adopted are not able to differentiate
between UPI and Digital Rupee from user’s perspective. The
government could bring more awareness and education about
this technology for widespread adoption.

B. Motivation for CBDC in India
The key motivations for introducing CBDC in India are
as follows


India being at the forefront of digital adoption was also
exploring its version of CBDC. The Reserve Bank of India
(RBI), which is the central bank of India, proposed CBDC
in January 2017 and launched a pilot project at the end of
2022 called Digital Rupee (e₹). RBI launched two types of
CBDC namely Wholesale CBDC (CBDC-W) or Digital
Rupee for Wholesale (e₹-W) and Retail CBDC (CBDC-R)
or Digital Rupee for Retail (e₹-R). CBDC-W (e₹-W) is
designed mainly for interbank transfers and wholesale
transactions and is proposed to be used by selected financial
institutes. CBDC-R (e₹-R) on the other hand is designed for
all retail transactions including consumers, businesses, all
private sector, and non-financial consumers.

C. Goal of CBDC in India
The goal of the Digital Rupee is to offer another choice
alongside existing forms of money. It is like banknotes;
however, it is likely to be cheaper, simpler, and quicker. It
also has all the transactional benefits and would provide
safer money payments and settlements since it’s a direct
liability of the RBI.[1].

The conceptual framework, motivation, design,
technology consideration, policy implications, etc. of
CBDC are detailed in RBI’s “Concept Note on Central Bank
Digital Currency” report published on October 7th, 2022.[1]

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
A. Implementation challenges on the CBDC concept
Various studies have been conducted at the conceptual
stage of CBDC from Indian perspectives. [2] One such
study was done in 2011 where conceptual issues concerning
monetary sovereignty, national sovereignty, and
developmental aspects were discussed. Another study was
conducted on business and technological risks/challenges
with CBDC implementation. [3]

D. Policy implications
CBDC is considered to not alter the basics of monetary
policy mechanics in India, however considered to be an
enabler of timely transmission of monetary policies. The
monetary policy implications potentially depend on CBDC
design and usage like whether it would be remunerated or
non-remunerated, widely adopted as physical currency or
limited to wholesale customers, anonymity like a physical
currency, or identifiable ownership.

B. Technology challenges
India still has an evolving digital infrastructure and
concepts like the Digital Rupee need some solid
infrastructure to be a successful project. One study was
conducted on the evolution of fifth-generation mobile
technology or simply 5G with CBDC for faster blockchain
transactions. [4]

E. Technology
The technology behind the Digital Rupee in India, or
Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC), involves several
components that ensure security, efficiency, and
accessibility. The major technology backbone is blockchain
which stores data in chronological order and data could not
be modified or deleted without consensus from the
blockchain network.

Another study was done on the various technology
stacks available like blockchain, POS, or POW mechanisms
etc, with the members implementing the digital rupee and
the advantages and disadvantages, and timing the launch
based on specific indices that are available. [5]

The design choices of technology and infrastructure
behind CBDC are kept flexible and open-ended by RBI
between centrally controlled databases or a distributed
ledger.

C. Impact on the economy
Studies have also been conducted on CBDC's impact on
the economy of a country. CBDCs impact on United States
banking and monetary policy was researched and found
potential in reducing deposit frictions and improving
financial inclusion, welfare and aid economic activities. [6]

F. CBDC vs Cryptocurrency
Both CBDC and cryptocurrency offer innovation in
digital finance however they follow different needs and
philosophies. The technology behind both are similar i.e.
blockchain and cryptographic techniques.

D. Adoption
Studies are conducted on the adoption of various
CBDCs in many countries. One was conducted in the
Netherlands with representative participants from Dutch
consumers and found high potential in CBDC adoption. The
research was done on CBDC Current and Saving accounts
opened in Dutch Banks and figured interest rates provided
in such accounts play a major factor in its adoption. [7]

Cryptocurrencies were intended to decentralize and have
independence from government control. CBDC, on the
other hand, is a digital currency regulated by any country's
central bank. It is intended to replace physical currency.
G. CBDC vs UPI
In India, one more popular means of digital payment is
UPI, which is a real-time payment system that transfers
money from one bank account to another. It is a facilitator
of transactions rather than a digital currency.

One interesting study was made between emerging and
developed countries with CBDC adoption. Wholesale
CBDCs were widely adopted in developed economies for
cross-border payments, whereas Retail CBDCs were more
adopted in emerging countries with low financial inclusion
and huge informal markets. [8]

On the other hand, CBDCs are digital currencies
providing an alternative to physical ones. There are no banks
involved in transfers, money is directly loaded into a wallet
and could be transferred to another wallet like cash. The
Indian government has also made interoperable between
UPI apps and CBDC wallet which makes it more
convenient.

Another research was done in the Netherlands on the
implication of retail CBDC on households and corporations
and found competitiveness to be critical for initial
adoption.[9]

H. Research focus
This research focuses on the Retail version of CBDC
also called Retail CBDC or Digital Rupee for Retail (e₹-R).

CBDC adoption impact based on the Hofstede Cultural
Framework was also studied. Countries with more power
distance, long-term oriented, and masculinity cultures are
more likely to adopt CBDC much earlier. Whereas countries
with a cultural inclination towards avoiding uncertainty
were not eager to adopt CBDC. [10]

For this research, we are capturing participants'
perspectives regarding adoption, awareness, their sentiment
towards this form of currency, and concerns/challenges that
are limiting their adoption. Also, the research captures
users’ opinions on government involvement in promoting
digital currency and the perceived role in addressing the
challenges through surveys and questionnaires.

The public’s perception was studied using social media
data between May 2012 and April 2022 using text mining
algorithms and found positive sentiment. Technology
awareness, inflation, and government performance were
major influencers of perception. [11]

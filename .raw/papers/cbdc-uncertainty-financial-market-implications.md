The current state of the art in the central bank digital currency (CBDC) literature views indexes constructed from
digital currency news to be fully informed about CBDC uncertainty and its impact on the financial system. We
argue that the hedging behavior of participants in the currency futures market could be more informative than
CBDC uncertainty news in the presence of limited risk absorption capacity in futures markets. We show that the
hedging factor has a statistically significant effect on financial market risk aversion and measures of uncertainty.
The hedging behavior of currency futures market participants is informative of agents' reactions to the news and
central bank policies around CBDC. Our results also show that CBDC uncertainty is a significant risk transmitter
in the financial system. Hence, this characteristic makes the hedging factor even more important because it can
directly impact risk aversion via its moderating effects, which later influence CBDC uncertainty.

1. Introduction
How do changes in central bank digital currency (CBDC) uncertainty
affect macroeconomic quantities and asset returns in financial markets?
Bekaert, Engstrom, and Xu (2022) state that because of the counter­
cyclicality associated with risk aversion, uncertainty shocks can increase
risk and risk aversion when consumption growth is falling. However, we
show that standard futures market hedging behavior minimizes CBDC
uncertainty. Under a habit-based explanation of CBDC risk premium, we
argue that the hedging factor influences CBDC uncertainty via its effect
on risk aversion. Hence, the hedging factor of risk-averse agents matters
to macroeconomic outcomes. To further illustrate the importance of the
hedging factor under a habit-based framework, it is instructive to
consider that the exchange rates of CBDCs, like that of fiat currencies,
carry consumption growth risks and a requirement by home country
investors for a positive CBDC risk premium (Verdelhan, 2010). Along
these lines, Backus, Foresi, and Telmer (2001) develop a set of stylized
facts in which the CBDC risk premia could be written as the difference
between the higher moments of foreign and domestic pricing kernels,
which can be reduced to the conditional variances when pricing kernels
are conditionally lognormal. Verdelhan (2010) states that when the
domestic kernel has a relatively high conditional variance, the domestic
investor who is long in the foreign Treasury bills will receive a positive
risk premium. Consequently, for these investors under the habit
framework, when the conditional variances of the pricing kernel are

large in bad times, consumption is close to the habit level, and risk
aversion is high, they will be net long in their hedging strategies.
Financial innovation is accelerating the development of various
central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) around the world, which signals
a new monetary era (Laboure, Müller, Heinz, Singh, & Kohling, 2021).1
However, this shift into CBDCs could have significant implications for
financial markets. For instance, Goodell and Shen (2021) argue that the
development and adoption of CBDC by major economies such as China
and the United States could alter the global payment systems and capital
flows. Le, Yarovaya, and Nasir (2021) question the future currency
hegemony's direction. Clearly, the adoption of CBDC will be monu­
mental. However, our understanding of its links to financial markets still
needs to be fully understood, given the critical role of the financial
market in price discovery and asset pricing. Hence, research on the
subject matter is both timely and necessary to aid global economic and
financial challenges facing policymakers and all stakeholders of eco­
nomic and financial stability. In this study, we document that though the
current state of the art would suggest using uncertainty indexes con­
structed from news to evaluate the impact of CBDC on several areas in
the finance literature, risk-averse financial market participants may
already be aware of the uncertainties associated with the adoption of
CBDCs as evidenced in the importance of their hedging positions.
Further motivation for this viewpoint is found in the literature doc­

Current finance research has viewed news regarding CBDC uncer­
tainty as being fully informative about future CBDC performance (Wang,
Lucey, Vigne, & Yarovaya, 2022a). Uncertainty measures have become
very popular in the literature because they can explain the risk premium
of financial assets (Jurado, Ludvigson, & Ng, 2015) and their effects on
the real economy (Bloom, 2009). The research, however, has been silent
on the role of the hedging behavior of risk-averse participants in, for
instance, the currency futures market seeking to hedge the uncertainties
associated with the respective trading currencies. Bekaert et al. (2022)
note that risk aversion influences the demand for insurance (hedging
activities), particularly during periods of elevated uncertainty. Theo­
retical motivation for this argument comes from the behavioral finance
literature that addresses the role of risk aversion and uncertainty in
determining macroeconomic quantities and asset returns. For instance, a
large literature in financial economics relies on economic uncertainty
and risk aversion as the main drivers of financial variables (see, for
instance, Bansal & Yaron, 2004; Bekaert et al., 2022). Bekaert et al.
(2022) show that risk aversion is substantially correlated with consumer
confidence measures. Along these lines, risk aversion highlights the need
for insurance, thus giving rise to the extensive economic literature on
health insurance, unemployment insurance, property insurance, flood
insurance, and other insurance (O'Donoghue & Somerville, 2018). Other
studies (e.g., Bali, Brown, & Tang, 2017; Campbell, Giglio, Polk, &
Turley, 2018; Dunbar & Owusu-Amoako, 2021; Merton, 1973) provide
theoretical and empirical evidence that investors create hedging stra­
tegies conditioned on the business cycle and financial uncertainties to
guard against potential welfare losses.
The motivation for our currency market hedging argument can be
found in a nascent strand in the monetary economics literature sug­
gesting a link between loose monetary policy and the risk aversion of
market participants (Bekaert et al., 2022). This research has initiated a
line of studies that explores the structural factors influencing risk
aversion (Adrian & Shin, 2009; Bekaert, Hoerova, & Lo Duca, 2013;
Rajan, 2006). Others, such as Miranda-Agrippino and Rey (2020) and
Rey (2016), have documented that risk aversion is a key transmission
vector of U.S. monetary policy in international finance. In our study, we
argue that the currency market hedging factor influences CBDC or cur­
rency market uncertainty via its moderating effects on risk aversion. Our
work not only creates a link between CBDC and traditional currency
markets but also helps to determine whether CBDCs share similarities
with traditional fiat currencies. We also establish a set of empirical
regularities that can be used as stylized facts and important inputs to
assess and develop theoretical models of CBDCs.
A U.S. government-issued CBDC, unlike the rapidly developing
decentralized cryptocurrencies, could take the form of a digital version
of the fiat currency, which would be a direct liability of the Fed, thereby
limiting liquidity and counterparty credit risk. However, as noted by the
Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve Systems (2022), whereas a U.
S.-backed CBDC would extend the public's access to banking and reduce
transaction costs, a CBDC could introduce systematic risks to the
financial system. For instance, the risk-free nature of CBDCs could
motivate consumers to redeem money market mutual funds (MMFs)
during a financial crisis and move funds into CBDC investments, thereby
aggravating liquidity issues in the financial market and creating disin­
termediation risks for MMFs. Given the importance of the currency as a

means of financial intermediation, it is essential to understand the
linkage of a CBDC to the existing financial system and whether the
existing futures market provides adequate risk management protections
for CBDC risk.
In this paper, we show that the hedging factor channel is a crucial
channel via which policy changes and financial news are transmitted via
exchange rates and a key source of predictability of movements in
financial asset returns. We also show several new facts consistent with
the view involving investors hedging behavior and news relating to
CBDC uncertainty in financial markets. First, movements in the net
positioning of agents in the currency futures market are significantly
correlated with movements in both the short-term interest rates, and
changes in the net positioning of agents in the currency futures market
are positively correlated with the Nominal Broad-Dollar Index (USD).
The USD is a tool used by the U.S. Federal Reserve Bank to evaluate the
strength of the U.S. dollar relative to other widely used currencies in
international trade. Instances in which the Nominal-Broad Dollar Index
is increasing are interpreted as the dollar strengthening relative to the
currencies of its main trading partners due to changes in U.S. domestic
policies. Additionally, periods of high U.S. economic activity tend to
coincide with an appreciation of the U.S. Dollar relative to the currencies
of the U.S. trading partners, low bond market returns, and high stock
returns. The fact that changes in the net positioning of agents in the
currency futures market are pro-cyclical is surprising as this finding is
new.
Second, we analyze the interlinkages and the return spillover effect
among CBDC uncertainty, crypto uncertainty, and the financial system
comprising the equity, bond, commercial banking system, and the ex­
change rate of the U.S. and its major trading partners (EUR - Euro, JPY –
Japanese Yen, GBP – British Pound, and CAD – Canadian Dollar) by
using a Vector Autoregression (VAR) and the spillover index of Diebold
and Yilmaz (2012). we find that central bank's digital currency uncer­
tainty (CBDCUI) proxied by the Wang et al. (2022a) central bank digital
currency uncertainty index (CBDCUI) is a significant risk transmitter in
the financial system. This finding is consistent with the views of the
Federal Reserve Bank, which noted this potential concern.3 This is
important since the USD is also a risk transmitter while the spot currency
exchange of its trading partners is the primary shock recipient.
Third, changes in the hedging behavior of currency market partici­
pants do hedge uncertainty in financial markets. Our primary evidence
is on CBDCUI and UCRY uncertainty (cryptocurrency uncertainty index
– Lucey, Vigne, Yarovaya, & Wang, 2021), which is ideal for testing our
hypothesis, which examines the links between CBDCs and existing
financial markets. In particular, we show that information on the un­
certainty arising from news coverage (Baker, Bloom, & Davis, 2016;
Huang & Luk, 2020; Lucey et al., 2021) and public attention to a
financial objective (He, Qin, & Zhang, 2021; Smales, 2022; Wang, 2022;
Wang et al., 2022a) is imputed in the hedging behavior of futures market
participants, acting to reduce currency risk. We find that a percent in­
crease in the hedging factor is associated with a 0.035% reduction in
CBDC uncertainty per week (or a 1.8% reduction per year). Noteworthy,
the coefficient of the hedging factor is positive with CBDC attention
(Wang et al., 2022a, CBDCAI index), indicating that attention to such a
financial objective would be welcome by the market. Interestingly, high
net positioning in the currency futures market also predicts a reduction
in the cryptocurrency and the broader financial market, implying that
the CBDC and cryptocurrency markets are linked to the existing finan­
cial market.

The CBDC exchange rate will depend on foreign and consumption
growth shocks in this setup. In this setup, Verdelhan (2010) argues that
when the domestic economy receives a negative (positive) consumption
growth shock, the exchange rate depreciates (appreciates), lowering

the domestic investor to expect a positive risk premium. As a result, risk
premia should be high when real interest rates are low. This negative
relationship between the currency risk premia and interest rates is
observed in currency markets for both nominal and real interest rates. In
equity markets, the evidence pertains to nominal interest rates (Ver­

rates and excess equity returns. Since Bekaert et al. (2022) and others
have argued that the risk-aversion of market participants drives the
demand for insurance or hedging instruments, we expect the currency
market hedging factor will be inverse with the currency market risk
premia.
In this study, we argue that hedging by risk-averse agents directly
influences risk aversion and subsequently affects CBDCUI uncertainty
via the CBDC risk premium channel. This argument has been put for­
ward for traditional assets such as equity and bonds (Bekaert et al.,
2022; Bretscher, Hsu, & Tamoni, 2022). Forward-looking market par­
ticipants are attentive to the news in the currency market and react to
any news that fuels currency market uncertainty. We premise that the
hedging factor moderates risk aversion's response to currency market
uncertainty. Hence, this forward-looking hedging factor can shed light
on future realizations of currency market uncertainty and market
attention to an economic objective. Our study adds to the CBDC litera­
ture in several ways. First, this area is essential for future research
because there needs to be more evidence as to whether agents' behavior
in futures markets appropriately captures the uncertainty and attention
around the introduction and adoption of CBDCs considering futures
markets' forward-looking properties. Second, it can help elucidate
CBDCs' impact on financial markets and uncertainty in predicting
financial asset returns.

2. Background literature
Broadly speaking, Wang et al. (2022a) identify five budding strands
of academic literature about CBDCs. The first strand attempts to explain
the potential advantages and risks of adopting CBDCs (Allen, Gu, &
Jagtiani, 2022; Cunha, Melo, & Sebastiao, 2021; Li & Huang, 2021;
Masciandaro, 2018; Yao, 2018a). Studies in this strand generally find
that adopting CBDCs would be positive for the banking systems. The
second strand examines CBDC's potential adoption of blockchain tech­
nology to mitigate settlement risk (Lee, Son, Park, Lee, & Jang, 2021)
and its implementation by regional central banks (Wagner, Bruggink, &
Benevelli, 2021; Yao, 2018b). Motivated by the second strand of liter­
ature, the third strand examines CBDC's security and privacy issues
(Borgonovo, Caselli, Cillo, Masciandaro, & Rabitti, 2021; Fu, Xu, Li, &
Ma, 2019; Pocher & Veneris, 2021; Tronnier, 2021). The studies in this
strand document the significance of anonymity in increasing the adop­
tion of CBDC. This may be an essential factor for the unbanked popu­
lation. For greater adoption, work in this area advocates for a balance
between privacy and transparency, which does not compromise
accountability.
The fourth strand, arguably the most nascent in this growing field,
explores the impacts of CBDCs on monetary policy (Tronnier, Recker, &
Hamm, 2020; Meaning, Dyson, Barker, & Clayton, 2021; Stevens, 2021;
Davoodalhosseini, 2021). These studies are motivated by CBDCs' com­
plementary benefits to existing monetary policy. This area of the liter­
ature reports that monetary policy can, in general, continue to operate
under a CBDC framework as under current fiat policies, providing ad­
justments to the price and quantity of the CBDC. However, it is believed
that CBDC will strengthen the monetary transmission mechanism and
transform the financial landscape once fully adopted. The fifth strand of
the literature investigates the link between CBDCs and the banking
sector (Fernandez-Villaverde, Sanches, Schilling, & Uhlig, 2021; Wil­
liamson, 2021). In general, this strand of the literature indicates a model
in which CBDC would allow central banks to have greater autonomy
over the banking system, which could impact the role of commercial
banks because CBDC could grant central banks a monopoly over bank
deposits.
From our literature review, there is a gap in the research to uncover
the links of CBDC to existing financial markets. In fact, others also argue
that more work is needed for a better understanding of this linkage
(Wang et al., 2022a). Building on the fourth strand of the literature, the
objective of our paper is to advance our understanding of CBDC's
connection to financial markets. We address the calls for this type of
research by demonstrating that the impact of uncertainty arising from
news coverage (Baker et al., 2016; Huang & Luk, 2020; Lucey et al.,
2021) and public attention to a financial objective (He et al., 2021;
Smales, 2022; Wang et al., 2022a) is factored in the hedging behavior of
futures market participants. To achieve this objective, we begin with the
assumption that expectations are rational. This allows us to demonstrate
that market participants' standard futures market hedging behavior is
both informative about risk aversion and currency market uncertainty
and connects CBDC to the existing financial market. The long-standing
forward premium anomaly (Uncovered interest parity condition – UIP
puzzle) suggests that the slope coefficient of the exchange rate on in­
terest rates does not match the empirical data (see discussion in Ver­
delhan, 2010). Hence, we use a habit-based explanation (Campbell &
Cochrane, 1999) to examine the impacts of standard futures market
hedging on CBDC uncertainty. Following the theoretical framework
developed by Verdelhan (2010), we argue that during periods of
heightened domestic economic uncertainty (characterized by bad eco­
nomic news), when domestic consumption is close to the habit level, a
representative investor will be highly risk averse. When the domestic

3.3. Methodology
To evaluate and explain the connectedness or inter-linkage between
CBDC and the financial system, the study's methodology consists of two
key elements: OLS estimation and testing of the predictability of the
currency hedging factor, and the Diebold and Yilmaz (2009, 2012)
connectivity index, which is VaR-based. The DY connectedness index is
used to evaluate the potential spillover of systematic risk associated with
CBDC uncertainty and its connectedness to the commercial banking
sector – MSCI_bank, the equity market - MSCI_E, the global bond marketFTSE_B, and the currency exchange market (GBP, JPY, CAD, EUR) and
the Nominal Broad-Dollar Index (USD), used by policymakers to mea­
sure the value of the U.S. dollar relative to other widely used currencies
in international trade.
It is noteworthy that the related literature has identified the DY
spillover index as a popular tool for evaluating the spillover effects be­
tween financial markets in the time domain. The methodology is
attractive for our study because identification of the connectedness and
risk spillovers between financial markets is important for determining
the source of systemic risk (Diebold & Yılmaz, 2014). Additionally,
Wang (2022) argues that studies examining systematic risk contagion
and spillover can yield useful information on transmission channels, risk
transmitters, and receivers. Further, Hamill, Li, Pantelous, Vigne, and
Waterworth (2021) state that the outcome of these models could assist
policymakers in developing policy tools for real-time monitoring and in
their policy response functions geared toward financial stability. How­
ever, despite its popularity, the DY spillover index has several weak­
nesses worth mentioning. Wang, Wei, Lucey, and Su (2023) and
Antonakakis, Chatziantoniou, & Gabauer, 2020 point out that a down­
side of the well-known DY spillover model is its static spillover analysis.

3.2. Construction of the currency market hedging factor
The Commodity Futures Trading Commission's (CFTC) commitment
of traders (COT) report data is covered by its Regulation 18.04, requiring
market participants to complete Form 40, which reports on traders' open
futures positions on each Tuesday of each week. The aggregated results
are later reported publicly with a 3-day lag on the CFTC's website.

based on their intended use of the futures contracts. Speculators include
market makers such as large banks and securities dealers who use fu­

Market participants on the “buy-side” generally include investors who
use futures for investment and hedging purposes, such as institutional
investors, exporters, and producers. In this study, we assume that market
participants will be net long currency futures when they anticipate
strong future economic growth and vice-versa. This is because of strong

0.153 and a heteroskedasticity-consistent t-statistic of 3.38. This means
that a 1% increase in the currency hedging factor increases expected
stock market returns by 0.153% per week. This positive effect between
the hedging factor and the returns in the equity market may arise
because of one or both of two reasons. First, the domestic currency can
appreciate relative to other currencies because of increased demand for
the domestic currency, or the central bank increases short-term rates,
which reduces the domestic money supply. In either case, this appreci­
ation in the domestic currency will lead to an increase in stock prices
since it now costs more to purchase the stocks. Consequently, rising
(declining) stock prices are linked to an appreciation (depreciation) in
the local currency. Column 2 of Table 5 similarly reports that the
UCRY_Po index predicts equity market returns significantly. Uncertainty
in crypto markets may force investors to seek similar risk assets such as
equities.
In Columns 3, 4, 6, and 7 of Table 5, we find that UCRY_Px, CBDCUI,
Vix, and EPU do not have forecasting power for expected stock returns.
However, CBDCAI indicates a positive economic and significant effect

study. We show that after controlling for all measures of uncertainty,
including the CBDC measures, the hedging factor remains statistically
and economically significant. The results also indicate that the positive
coefficient of the hedging factor offsets the negative effect of the impacts
of CBDCUI. The results show that the hedging factor has a coefficient
estimate of 0.206 and a heteroskedasticity-consistent t-statistic of 3.33.

work also introduces a new approach that establishes empirical risk
management regularities that can be used as stylized facts and essential
inputs to assess and develop theoretical models of CBDC. The study
shows that the hedging behavior of agents in the currency futures
market contains information that is not fully revealed by news alone.
The idea that the hedging behavior of market participants could be more
informative than news is entirely new. It offers a richer understanding of
the interlinkage of CBDCs to the financial markets.
The adoption of CBDC will undoubtedly introduce new uncertainties
in the financial system. Prior studies have shown that digital currency
news is a significant indicator of CBDC risk (Wang et al., 2022a: Lucey
et al., 2021). These empirical models generally assume that CBDC news
is vital in determining the uncertainty around this new digital asset and
its potential impact on the economy. For instance, Wang et al. (2022)
document that good news around important digital currency events can
significantly reduce CBDC uncertainty around these assets. However,
our findings suggest that the hedging behavior of currency futures
market participants is informative of agents' reactions to the news and
central bank policies around CBDC. We argue that hedging behavior is
more informative than news because it reflects agents' time-varying risk
aversion.
We show that hedging behavior in the currency futures market
significantly hedges CBDC risk in the Commercial Banking Sector, The
bond, equity, foreign exchange and cryptocurrency markets. The results
remained robust even after controlling for other well-known measures of
financial market uncertainty (Vix), Economic Uncertainty (EPU), and
cryptocurrency market uncertainty UCRY_Po, UCRY_Px, thus validating
the empirical findings. These findings benefit policymakers by con­
firming that implementing CBDC will introduce significant systematic
risk in financial markets. However, the linkage of CBDC to the financial
market allows hedging activity of agents in the currency futures market
to immunize these potential risks. Hence it will be essential to ensure
continued liquidity in currency markets and access to currency futures
contracts as agents seek to hedge CBDC's uncertainty.

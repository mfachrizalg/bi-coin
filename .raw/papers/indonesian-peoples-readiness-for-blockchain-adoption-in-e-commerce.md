Abstract— Indonesia's e-commerce industry is rapidly
growing, with a notable rise in transaction volumes by 2022.
This study explores blockchain adoption in retail e-commerce,
focusing on its potential to enhance efficiency and security. It
examines the use of blockchain, particularly Central Bank
Digital Currency (CBDC), which uses a private blockchain
regulated by central authorities, unlike public cryptocurrencies.
Using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the study
assesses public readiness for blockchain adoption through
online questionnaires distributed to Indonesian e-commerce
users. The results highlight key factors such as security,
transparency, cost efficiency, and transaction speed that
significantly influence consumer trust and adoption. The study
also identifies regulatory challenges and infrastructural
readiness as crucial aspects in the adoption process.
Furthermore, this research offers practical recommendations
for policymakers, industry leaders, and regulators to enhance
digital infrastructure, improve cybersecurity measures, and
establish a regulatory framework for secure blockchain
adoption. By addressing these factors, Indonesia can build a
more efficient and transparent digital economy, ensuring
sustainable growth in its e-commerce ecosystem.

However, Indonesia faces regulatory barriers to

regulated by central authorities, ensuring compliance with

benefits of security and transparency. This research aims to

fraud risks, and inefficiencies in Indonesia's e-commerce
sector. By leveraging blockchain’s ability to bypass
conventional telecommunications infrastructure, such as
tower installations in remote areas, it presents a costeffective solution to Indonesia's infrastructural challenges.
The findings of this study will provide insights for

I. INTRODUCTION
In the era of globalization, Indonesia's e-commerce
industry has witnessed exponential growth, fundamentally
altering consumer shopping behavior. According to data from
Momentum Works, one of Indonesia's leading e-commerce
platforms reached a remarkable transaction value of IDR
278.5 trillion in 2022 [1]. This rapid growth underscores the
importance of robust transaction infrastructure to ensure
seamless operations in the e-commerce ecosystem. However,
despite its potential, the e-commerce sector in Indonesia
faces persistent challenges, including slow transaction
speeds, high security risks, and inefficiencies in payment
processing systems. These issues are exacerbated by network
congestion, cross-platform incompatibility, and delays in
payment processing between financial systems. Addressing
these challenges is critical for sustainable e-commerce
growth.
Blockchain technology, as a key innovation in digital
transactions, offers promising solutions to these issues. By
leveraging a decentralized system, blockchain enhances
transparency and security while reducing transaction
inefficiencies. Each transaction is permanently recorded on a
distributed ledger, minimizing fraud risks, and expediting
payment processes. Compared to existing payment
technologies, such as digital wallets and credit cards,
blockchain stands out for its unique capabilities. Digital

Moreover, blockchain’s role as a digital infrastructure is
intricately linked to technological advancements. As a
distributed ledger technology, blockchain integrates
seamlessly with other digital systems, enabling real-time data
synchronization and secure communication across platforms.
For instance, blockchain can complement existing ecommerce platforms by automating processes such as
payment settlements, fraud detection, and inventory
management. This capability aligns with the broader
technological evolution of Indonesia's digital economy,
emphasizing the importance of adopting innovative systems
to drive efficiency and reliability.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
A. E-commerce
E-commerce is a platform that facilitates online buying
and selling transactions. However, it's important to note that
e-commerce encompasses more than just financial
transactions. It also includes nonfinancial interactions
between the company and its customers [4]. A transaction in
e-commerce is classified as electronic commerce today if the
World Wide Web is significantly utilized at any phase of the
transaction process [5], [6]. In addition, e-commerce currently
faces two major challenges: the demand for a seamless
transaction experience and the global push for
decarbonization [7]. E-commerce has a significant impact on
the business world today by transforming transactions and
changing global markets. E-commerce makes it easier for
users to find the items they need. It involves a variety of
activities such as online shopping, auctions, digital content
downloads, electronic transactions, and ticket purchasing.
With everything available digitally, users can shop from any
location at any time using devices connected to the internet
[8].
E-commerce offers a variety of payment options,
including credit cards, digital wallets, and other secure
electronic methods that facilitate transactions by encrypting
data to ensure security [9]. It has been particularly
advantageous for small businesses, allowing them to access a
wider audience. Establishing consumer trust and adopting
strong cybersecurity practices are crucial for the expansion of
e-commerce. The convenience, accessibility, and global reach
of e-commerce have reshaped the modern business landscape.
As technology evolves and consumer behavior changes, ecommerce is projected to continue its rapid growth, bringing
both opportunities and challenges for businesses in the digital
era.
B. Blockchain
Blockchain provides a decentralized solution for secure
and transparent data management, enhancing trust and
security in e-commerce by enabling immutable transaction
records. Successful examples in developing countries include
Kenya’s Twiga Foods, improving payment transparency and
supply chain efficiency, and India’s blockchain-based public
distribution systems for secure benefit transfers [26].
Blockchain's ability to enhance transparency and cost
efficiency is evident in these cases, even with challenges like
limited infrastructure and digital literacy. In Indonesia,
blockchain is still in its early stages but has significant
potential to optimize transaction security and operational
efficiency in e-commerce [27].
Blockchain outperforms traditional payment methods,
such as e-wallets and credit cards, by reducing costs,
eliminating intermediaries, and enhancing security with
decentralized, tamper-proof ledgers [28]. In Indonesia, the
implementation of Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC)
offers a regulated blockchain solution, ensuring both security
and legal compliance. Unlike cryptocurrencies, CBDCs are
regulated by the central bank, ensuring a more efficient
payment system while maintaining the role of traditional
banks. China’s successful Digital Yuan implementation
demonstrates how CBDCs can streamline payment systems
and offer greater efficiency while preserving banking
functions [29].

Blockchain helps address e-commerce challenges in
Indonesia by improving transparency, reducing transaction
costs, and enhancing security against cyberattacks, as
demonstrated by successful remittance platforms in the
Philippines [30]. However, challenges such as scalability,
regulatory uncertainty, and public trust must be addressed.
The adoption of blockchain, particularly through CBDC,
promises enhanced security and efficiency for digital
transactions, but efforts in infrastructure development,
education, and regulatory support are needed to realize its full
potential. Collaborative efforts among government, industry,
and academia are essential to advancing blockchain adoption
and overcoming these barriers.
C. Technology Acceptance Model
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) is a
framework utilized to examine the factors that impact the
acceptance of a system or an information system. The model
highlights two main aspects, namely Perceived Usefulness
and Perceived Ease of Use, which influence users' intention in
adopting new technology. Various studies have shown that
perceived usefulness and ease of use play a significant role in
the adoption of blockchain technology. One of them is a study
which discusses blockchain adoption in the financial services
sector [24]. In addition, the application of TAM to ecommerce technology has also integrated the concept of trust
in the analysis of e-commerce acceptance [25]. From these
various findings, the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
has been validated as an effective framework for evaluating
the acceptance of a system or information technology.
Consequently, TAM is suggested as a research variable for
studying the adoption of new technologies.

V. CONCLUSION
The research confirms that Blockchain Cost Efficiency,
Transaction Security, Transaction Speed, and Transparency
significantly impact Consumer Trust, which, in turn,
positively influences E-Commerce Adoption. This study
evaluates the readiness of Indonesian society to adopt
blockchain for e-commerce transactions, particularly through
the use of Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC). Its goal is
to raise awareness about the potential development of
payment technology in Indonesia, especially if CBDC is
implemented. Key factors such as Blockchain Transaction
Security, Transparency, Cost Efficiency, and Transaction
Speed were found to significantly influence Consumer Trust,
which is essential for adopting e-commerce technology.
However, certain indicators (BTS3, BT2, BT4, BCE3, BS3,
and CT4) were excluded, as they reflected individual
preferences rather than the broader readiness for blockchain
adoption. The research shows that blockchain technology has
the potential to enhance the efficiency, transparency, and
security of e-commerce transactions, especially with CBDC.
Blockchain addresses issues like fraud and transaction
inefficiencies by providing verifiable, transparent records. It
also improves transaction speed and reduces intermediary
costs, further boosting cost-efficiency. Adopting blockchain
requires readiness in public understanding, digital
infrastructure, and regulatory frameworks. Consumers' trust
in blockchain is influenced by its security, transparency,
cost-efficiency, and speed, making it essential for enhancing
e-commerce infrastructure.
For the government, the key practical implications
involve developing secure digital infrastructure and
establishing robust regulatory frameworks to support
blockchain adoption. This includes implementing secure
storage solutions through custodian banks, creating reliable
wallet systems, and ensuring regulations like CASE
(Centralized Authentication and Secure Exchange) to protect
digital assets. Regulatory bodies such as OJK (Otoritas Jasa
Keuangan) and BAPPEBTI (Badan Pengawas Perdagangan
Berjangka Komoditi) should oversee these efforts, ensuring
compliance with financial regulations and safeguarding
consumer interests. For the industry, blockchain adoption
can reduce fraud, improve transaction efficiency, and
enhance transparency in supply chains. Companies should
invest in blockchain solutions to streamline payment
systems, lower operational costs, and secure customer data,

which will improve business efficiency and increase
consumer confidence in using CBDC. The research indicates
that Indonesians are ready for CBDC adoption within
blockchain-based
e-commerce
systems.
Blockchain
technology has the potential to transform Indonesia’s digital
economy, offering long-term benefits in security, efficiency,
and sustainability. Successful integration requires
collaboration between the government and industry to
address challenges in infrastructure development, education,
and regulation. Future research should distinguish between
those with blockchain experience and those without avoiding
contradictory results. It should also explore how education
can accelerate blockchain adoption and its potential to
reshape Indonesia’s e-commerce landscape. In conclusion,
blockchain provides a robust solution to issues like fraud and
inefficiency in e-commerce. By focusing on technological
infrastructure, regulatory frameworks, and consumer
education, Indonesia can effectively integrate blockchain
into its financial system, creating a secure, efficient, and
transparent e-commerce environment.
